\begin{abstract}
The source paper conjectures that its $\GSp$ system has no $\KMS_\beta$ state at
$\beta=1$ or $3$.  Its linear-subspace construction indeed cannot produce these
exponents.  We use instead the nonlinear hypersurface of ordered symplectically
orthogonal pairs.  At every finite prime, the canonical hypersurface measure is
Radon, has finite nonzero mass on the integral locus, and transforms under a
symplectic similitude $g$ by $|\lambda(g)|_p^3$.  The normalized restricted
product therefore transforms under rational $g$ by $\lambda(g)^{-3}$.  Combining
it with Haar measure on $\PGSp(\mathbb R)$ gives exactly the normalized measure
required for a $\KMS_3$ state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim and source reduction}

Let $V=\mathbb Q^4$ carry its standard nondegenerate alternating form
$\omega(v,w)=v^{\mathsf T}Jw$.  Write $\lambda(g)$ for the multiplier of
$g\in\GSp$, so that
\[
  \omega(gv,gw)=\lambda(g)\omega(v,w),
  \qquad \det g=\lambda(g)^2.
\]
Let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb Z)$ and $P=\PGSp(\mathbb R)$.

Proposition 2.12 of the source paper identifies $\KMS_\beta$ states with
$\Gamma$-invariant Radon measures $\mu$ on
\[
  P\times\MSp(\Afinite)
\]
whose induced measure on
$\Gamma\backslash(P\times\MSp(\Zhat))$ has mass one and which satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-scaling}
  \mu(gB)=\lambda(g)^{-\beta}\mu(B)
  \qquad(g\in\GSp^+(\mathbb Q)).
\end{equation}
The source then conjectures nonexistence at $\beta=1,3$; see its Remark 1 on
printed page 38.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The $\GSp$ system of arXiv:2211.07778 admits a $\KMS_3$ state.  Consequently,
the source conjecture that there are no $\KMS_\beta$ states in the two cases
$\beta=1,3$ is false.
\end{theorem}

The point is that linear subspaces are unnecessarily restrictive.  A
codimension-one nonlinear cone supplies precisely the missing exponent.

\section{The local symplectic hypersurface measure}

Fix a prime $p$ and give $\Qp^4$ additive Haar measure with
$\vol(\Zp^4)=1$.  Put
\[
  \Sigma_p=\{(v,w)\in\Qp^4\times\Qp^4:\omega(v,w)=0\}.
\]
For $\Phi\in C_c(\Qp^4\times\Qp^4)$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-measure}
  \nu_p(\Phi)
  =\lim_{n\to\infty}p^n
    \int \Phi(v,w)\,
    \mathbf 1_{p^n\Zp}\!\left(\omega(v,w)\right)\,dv\,dw.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local}
The limit in \eqref{eq:local-measure} exists and defines a positive Radon
measure supported on $\Sigma_p$.  Moreover,
\begin{align}
  \nu_p(\Sigma_p\cap(\Zp^4)^2)
    &=c_p:=\frac{1-p^{-4}}{1-p^{-3}}, \label{eq:cp}\\
  \nu_p(gE)&=|\lambda(g)|_p^3\nu_p(E)
    \qquad(g\in\GSp(\Qp)). \label{eq:local-scaling}
\end{align}
The locus where $v,w$ are linearly dependent has $\nu_p$-measure zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed nonzero $v$, the map $w\mapsto\omega(v,w)$ is a nonzero linear
functional.  Formula \eqref{eq:local-measure} is its Haar disintegration at
zero.  On compact sets its density is bounded by a constant times
$\|v\|_p^{-1}$.  This is locally integrable in four variables: on the shells
$p^k\Zp^4\setminus p^{k+1}\Zp^4$ the relevant sum is a constant multiple of
$\sum_{k\geq0}p^{-3k}$.  Dominated convergence first for compactly supported
locally constant functions, followed by the Riesz representation theorem,
gives a positive Radon measure.  A test function supported away from
$\omega=0$ eventually contributes zero, so the measure is supported on
$\Sigma_p$.

For the exact integral mass, let
$k(v)=\min_i v_p(v_i)$ for $v\in\Zp^4\setminus\{0\}$.  If $n>k(v)$, then
\[
 \vol\{w\in\Zp^4:\omega(v,w)\in p^n\Zp\}=p^{-(n-k(v))}.
\]
The shell $k(v)=k$ has volume $p^{-4k}(1-p^{-4})$.  Hence
\[
 c_p=(1-p^{-4})\sum_{k\geq0}p^{-4k}p^k
     =\frac{1-p^{-4}}{1-p^{-3}},
\]
which is finite and positive.

For \eqref{eq:local-scaling}, make the change of variables
$(v,w)=(gu,gz)$.  The ambient Jacobian is
$|\det g|_p^2=|\lambda(g)|_p^4$, while the delta approximation in
\eqref{eq:local-measure} contributes $|\lambda(g)|_p^{-1}$ because
$\omega(gu,gz)=\lambda(g)\omega(u,z)$.  Their product is
$|\lambda(g)|_p^3$.  Finally, for each $v\ne0$, the dependent vectors
$w\in\Qp v$ form a Haar-null subset of the three-dimensional hyperplane
$v^\perp$; integration in $v$ proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

Embed $\Sigma_p$ in the symplectic monoid by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding}
  \iota_p(v,w)=\begin{pmatrix} v&w&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where the displayed vectors are columns.  Since $\omega(v,w)=0$, one has
$\iota_p(v,w)^{\mathsf T}J\iota_p(v,w)=0$, so the multiplier is zero and
$\iota_p(v,w)\in\MSp(\Qp)$.  Also
$g\iota_p(v,w)=\iota_p(gv,gw)$; thus the support is left $\GSp$-stable.
It is nonlinear, and almost every matrix on it has rank two.

\section{Restricted product and proof of Theorem \ref{thm:main}}

Normalize $\bar\nu_p=c_p^{-1}\nu_p$.  By \eqref{eq:cp}, the compact open set
\[
 K_p=\Sigma_p\cap(\Zp^4)^2
\]
has $\bar\nu_p$-mass one.  The restricted product
\[
  \bar\nu_f=\mathop{\bigotimes\!'}_p\bar\nu_p
\]
is therefore a Radon measure on the finite-adelic hypersurface
\[
  \Sigma(\Afinite)=
  \{(v,w)\in\Afinite^4\times\Afinite^4:\omega(v,w)=0\}.
\]
Push it forward under the adelic version of \eqref{eq:embedding}, and denote
the resulting measure on $\MSp(\Afinite)$ by $\mu_f$.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integral-mass}
  \mu_f(\MSp(\Zhat))=1.
\end{equation}

For $g\in\GSp^+(\mathbb Q)$, only finitely many local factors move the
distinguished compact sets.  Lemma \ref{lem:local} and the product formula give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-scaling}
  \mu_f(gE)
   =\prod_p|\lambda(g)|_p^3\,\mu_f(E)
   =\lambda(g)^{-3}\mu_f(E).
\end{equation}
The equality is immediate on restricted cylinder sets and extends to Borel
sets by regularity.

Let $m_\infty$ be left Haar measure on $P$, normalized so that its induced
measure on $\Gamma\backslash P$ has mass one, and set
\[
  \mu=m_\infty\otimes\mu_f.
\]
This is a Radon measure on $P\times\MSp(\Afinite)$.  It is
$\Gamma$-invariant: the real factor is left invariant and
$\lambda(\gamma)=1$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$.  A fundamental domain for
$\Gamma\backslash P$, crossed with the integral finite-adelic locus, is a
fundamental domain for the diagonal action.  Equations
\eqref{eq:integral-mass} and the normalization of $m_\infty$ therefore give
\[
  \mu\bigl(\Gamma\backslash(P\times\MSp(\Zhat))\bigr)=1.
\]
The real Haar factor is unchanged by rational left translation, while
\eqref{eq:global-scaling} gives
\[
  \mu(gB)=\lambda(g)^{-3}\mu(B).
\]
Thus $\mu$ satisfies exactly \eqref{eq:source-scaling} with $\beta=3$.
Proposition 2.12 of the source paper now produces a $\KMS_3$ state, proving
Theorem \ref{thm:main}. \qed

\section{Audit, scope, and novelty status}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{No formal delta distribution is assumed.}  Equation
  \eqref{eq:local-measure} is a positive limit of ordinary Haar integrals;
  local integrability at the unique singular point is the convergent
  $\sum p^{-3k}$ estimate.
  \item \textbf{The exponent is exact.}  Two vectors contribute the ambient
  Jacobian $|\det g|_p^2=|\lambda(g)|_p^4$ and the single symplectic equation
  removes one factor $|\lambda(g)|_p$, leaving exponent $3$.
  \item \textbf{Normalization is local-to-global.}  Dividing by the explicit
  positive number $c_p$ makes every distinguished integral factor have mass
  one, so the restricted product is canonical up to the harmless normalization
  already fixed.
  \item \textbf{Scope.}  The packet proves existence at $\beta=3$ for the
  $\GSp$ system governed by Proposition 2.12.  It does not classify all
  $\KMS_3$ states, decide the separate $\beta=1$ case, or assert the analogous
  conclusion for the source's non-free Connes--Marcolli quotient.
\end{itemize}

A bounded novelty search on 26 August 2026 checked the four run indexes, the
exact conjecture phrase, the paper title, and arXiv searches combining
``$\GSp$'', ``$\KMS_3$'', and ``Siegel modular variety''.  No later arXiv paper
answering this exceptional-temperature conjecture was found.  This supports,
but does not certify, novelty.
