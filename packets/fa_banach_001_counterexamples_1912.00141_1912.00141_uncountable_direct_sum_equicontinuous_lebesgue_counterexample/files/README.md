# Counterexample packet: equicontinuous operator topology need not be Lebesgue

Status: `candidate_counterexample_likely_valid`

Source: Omid Zabeti, *AM-spaces from a locally solid vector lattice point of
view with applications*, arXiv:1912.00141, printed page 8, paragraph preceding
Corollary 7.

## Result

The general continuous-operator version of the source paper's Theorem 5 is
false.

Let `I` be uncountable and let `X = R^(I)` be the finitely supported functions,
with the locally solid topology generated by every weighted `l1` seminorm

```text
p_a(x) = sum_i a_i |x_i|,    a in [0,infinity)^I.
```

Then `X` has the AM and Levi properties. Let `Y = l1(I)`. It is order complete
and its norm topology has the Lebesgue property. For finite `F subset I`, let
`T_F` delete the coordinates in `F`. Each `T_F : X -> Y` is positive,
continuous, and order bounded, and `T_F` decreases to zero as `F` increases.
Nevertheless, the net does not converge to zero in the equicontinuous
convergence topology on `B_c^b(X,Y)`.

The obstruction is cardinal: a basic neighborhood of `X` uses only finitely
many weight functions. On an uncountable index set, their combined reciprocal
cannot tend to zero off finite sets. Thus every finite-complement projection
still maps some uniformly sized coordinate vector from that neighborhood to a
vector of fixed positive `l1` norm.

## Verification

The proof is non-computational. The audit checks separately that bounded
subsets of `X` have common finite support, that this yields AM and Levi, that
`l1(I)` is order complete and Lebesgue for arbitrary nets, that `T_F` decreases
to zero in the operator lattice, and that the defining quantifiers of
equicontinuous convergence fail for the norm unit ball of `Y`.

Human review should focus on the basic-neighborhood argument in the final
step and on interpreting "a version of Theorem 5" with exactly the hypotheses
printed in the source. The example is locally convex but deliberately
nonmetrizable, so it does not conflict with the source's Frechet corollary.
