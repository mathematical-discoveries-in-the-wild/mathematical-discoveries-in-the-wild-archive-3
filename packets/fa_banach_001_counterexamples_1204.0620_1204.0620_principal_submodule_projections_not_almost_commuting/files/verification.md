## Exact checks

- **Target:** PASS. The source explicitly proposes that the projections onto
  `[p]` and `[q]` may always almost commute in the Hardy, Bergman, or
  Drury-Arveson module.
- **Admissible polynomials:** PASS. `p=z_1` and `q=z_1+z_2` are polynomials in
  the source's ring `C[z_1,...,z_n]` for every `n>=3`.
- **Homogeneous reduction:** PASS. Principal submodules generated by linear
  homogeneous polynomials are graded, so their projections reduce each
  homogeneous degree.
- **Equal norms:** PASS. In all three modules, degree-`m` monomial norms have
  the form `c_m alpha!`; consequently `z_1 z_3^(m-1)` and
  `z_2 z_3^(m-1)` are orthogonal and have equal norm.
- **First quotient projection:** PASS. On `V_m`, the complement of `[z_1]` is
  exactly the second coordinate line.
- **Second quotient projection:** PASS. `z_1 z_3^(m-1)+z_2 z_3^(m-1)` lies in
  `[z_1+z_2]`, while the difference is orthogonal to the entire degree-`m`
  part of that submodule.
- **Matrix calculation:** PASS. The commutator is the skew matrix with
  off-diagonal entries `+/- 1/2`, hence has norm `1/2`.
- **Noncompactness:** PASS. Applying the commutator to one normalized monomial
  from each mutually orthogonal degree gives an orthonormal weakly-null
  sequence whose image norms are constantly `1/2`.
- **Computational dependency:** none; the calculation is exact.

## Scope

The example disproves the unrestricted “always” suggestion for every
dimension `n>=3` and for each of the three named modules. It does not decide
the corresponding question in dimensions one or two, nor does it contradict
the source's positive result under disjoint boundary zero varieties.
