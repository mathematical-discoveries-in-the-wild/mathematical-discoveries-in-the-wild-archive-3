\section{Source problem}

The source is R. D. Carmichael, S. Pilipovi\'{c}, and J. Vindas,
\emph{Vector valued Hardy spaces related to analytic functions having
distributional boundary values}, arXiv:1801.06709 (published 2019)
\cite{source}.  Theorems 4.2--4.4 establish Hardy-space membership and
Poisson representation for Hilbert-valued analytic functions in the range
$2\leq p\leq\infty$.  Section 5, PDF page 20, states:

\begin{quote}
For the cases $1\leq p<2$, results like Theorems 4.2, 4.3, and 4.4 require
separate proof techniques.  The authors propose to use Banach spaces having
Fourier type $p$, and say that future research will obtain those theorems in
the low-$p$ range.
\end{quote}

Thus the concrete target is to pass below $p=2$, with Fourier type suggested
as the additional Banach-space hypothesis.

\section{Explicit supporting answer}

The separate later paper by the same authors,
\emph{Note on vector valued Hardy spaces related to analytic functions having
distributional boundary values}, arXiv:2001.10021 (2020) \cite{support},
explicitly identifies the source and its Theorem 4.4.  Its introduction,
PDF page 1, says that the earlier paper obtained the vector-valued result only
for $2\leq p\leq\infty$ and only for Hilbert targets, and then states that the
aim of the note is to prove it for every $1\leq p\leq\infty$ and every Banach
space.

Theorem 1, on supporting PDF page 2, gives the promised statement.  If $X$ is
any Banach space, $C$ is a regular open convex cone, $p\in[1,\infty]$, and an
$X$-valued holomorphic function $F:T^C\to X$ has distributional boundary
value $f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n,X)$, then
\[
 F\in H^p(T^C,X),\qquad
 \|F\|_{H^p}=\|f\|_{L^p},
\]
and the proof identifies $F$ with the Poisson integral of $f$.
