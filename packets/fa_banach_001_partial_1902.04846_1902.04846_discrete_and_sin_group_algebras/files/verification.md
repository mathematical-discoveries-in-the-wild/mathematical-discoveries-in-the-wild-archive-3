# Verification Report

Candidate: arXiv:1902.04846, Question 3.7.

## Claim checked

For every locally compact SIN group `G`, `L^1(G)` is zero Jordan product
determined. Consequently `ell^1(G)` has this property for every discrete
group.

## Verdict

`likely valid`

## Step check

| Step | Status | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The diagonal difference in `A(T^2)` is approximable by separated-support pure tensors. | valid | A cutoff leaves a smooth function supported off the diagonal. Its removed part tends to zero in Wiener norm by an explicit `H^1 -> A(T)` bound. A finite product-cover, smooth partition, and absolutely convergent double Fourier series give the tensors. |
| The cyclic functional calculus `pi_s:A(T)->M(G)` is contractive. | valid | `pi_s(f)=sum_n fhat(n) delta_{s^n}` converges absolutely in measure norm and is a unital homomorphism, even when `s` has finite order. |
| A separated-support tensor maps to an anticommuting product. | valid | Choose smooth `q=1` on the first support and `q=-1` on the second. With `a=pi_s(q)` and `b=pi_s(g) delta_t pi_s(h)`, one has `ab=b` and `ba=-b`. |
| Every point-mass commutator is in `J(ell^1(G))`. | valid | The tensor map sends the diagonal difference to `[delta_s,delta_t]`; continuity and the preceding exact products place its limit in the closed subspace `J`. |
| Every discrete commutator is in `J`. | valid | The expansion `[p,q]=sum_{s,t}p(s)q(t)[delta_s,delta_t]` is absolutely convergent in `ell^1`, with total norm at most `2||p||_1||q||_1`. |
| A locally compact SIN group has a central contractive approximate identity. | valid | Shrinking relatively compact conjugation-invariant neighborhoods give normalized indicators. SIN groups are unimodular, these functions commute with all point masses and hence all measures, and the usual neighborhood argument gives an approximate identity. |
| The multiplier witness transfers into `L^1(G)` exactly. | valid | If multiplier `a` satisfies `ab=b`, `ba=-b`, then `a_i=a*e_i` lies in `L^1`; centrality gives `a_i b=b e_i` and `b a_i=-b e_i`. Thus `b e_i` is a defining generator of `J` and converges to `b`. |
| All SIN-group commutators are in `J`. | valid | First `[delta_s,k]` is in `J`; then `[f,k]=integral f(s)[delta_s,k] ds` is a Bochner integral of a norm-continuous `J`-valued function. |
| The source criterion applies. | valid | The source records that every `L^1(G)` is weakly amenable, has property B, and has a bounded approximate identity, and proves ZJPD iff all commutators lie in `J`. |

## Adversarial checks

- No spectral-synthesis theorem is assumed. The only approximation lemma used
  for `A(T^2)` is proved with a cutoff and Fourier series.
- The construction does not require the cyclic subgroup generated by `s` to
  be infinite or embedded faithfully in the circle calculus.
- In the discrete proof the smooth cutoff maps to an element of `ell^1(G)`,
  so the anticommuting pair is internal to the algebra.
- In the nondiscrete proof point masses and cyclic functions remain in the
  multiplier algebra, but multiplying by the central approximate identity
  produces internal witnesses before taking the limit.
- The Bochner integral is legitimate because left and right translation are
  norm-continuous on `L^1(G)`.
- The final criterion concerns the closed linear span `J`; it does not require
  `J` to be an ideal.

## Limitation and failed transfer

For a general locally compact group, an arbitrary bounded approximate
identity need not commute with `pi_s(q)`. Replacing `a` by `a*e_i` then does
not preserve `a_i b+b a_i=0` exactly. The proof therefore stops at SIN groups
and does not claim a full answer to the source question.
